What the tracker does each month

Four steps turn 500 daily news lines into ten storylines

1CollectEvery daily entry on Wikipedia's Current Events page, about 54,000 since 2018.Feb 2022: 543 events
2FilterCodex (an AI model) scores each event 0–3 for market relevance and keeps 2–3.Invasion of Ukraine: 3
3Group and followRelated events become one storyline with the same ID every month, labelled escalating, steady or easing.259 events → "Russia–Ukraine war", escalating
4Add reactionsOfficial actions from the US Federal Register, EU and UK sanctions lists, and the Fed, ECB and BoE.518 new EU sanctions listings
Check 1 · Is Codex giving the right labels?

Codex keeps the right news about 4 times in 5, twice as well as a keyword rule

A cheaper model trained on our own computer reached 0.65, so Codex stays as the filter.

How it's measured. 220 news items were labelled "matters for markets" or not by a second AI (Claude). The F1 score combines two things: not missing important news, and not keeping junk. 1.0 is perfect. A human check of these labels is still to come.

Check 2 · Does the tracker follow real conflict risk?

The tracker's conflict level rises and falls with an established risk index

Our tracker: conflict activityGeopolitical Risk index (military)

Both jump at the Ukraine invasion, the Gaza war and the Iran war, and stay quiet in between.

How it's measured. The Geopolitical Risk (GPR) index, from Federal Reserve economists Caldara and Iacoviello, measures how much major newspapers write about war and military threats each month. Our line is the number of conflict events the tracker kept, weighted by relevance. Each line is scaled to its own peak, so compare shapes, not heights.

Check 3 · Is the AI worth it over simple counting?

For the monthly total, simple word counting is almost as good; the AI's value is in the detail

Codex trackerKeyword count ("war", "missile" …)Wikipedia's "Armed conflicts" countPass mark 0.3

Codex passes in every period from 2018. Keyword counting comes close. What counting cannot give is named storylines, whether each is escalating or easing, and which government reacted.

How it's measured. For each period: when the risk index rose from one month to the next, did the method rise too? The score is a rank correlation: 1 means always in step, 0 means unrelated. The pass mark of 0.3 was written down before the test.

Check 4 · Is the history test peeking at the future?

Using Wikipedia as it looked the next day changes the result by at most 0.06

People edit Wikipedia after the event. Re-running on the version saved the day after each event gives the same picture, so later edits are not flattering our history.

How it's measured. Wikipedia keeps every saved version of every page. We downloaded each day's page as it stood the next day and re-ran the whole tracker. The limit, set in advance, was a change of at most 0.10.

Check 5 · Do government reactions line up?

US trade actions rise when trade-policy uncertainty rises

US tariff and trade-investigation actions per monthTrade Policy Uncertainty index

The 2018–19 US–China trade war and the 2025 tariff wave show up in both. Correlation within each period: 0.42.

How it's measured. Bars count significant tariff and trade-investigation notices in the US Federal Register, the government's official daily journal (Codex sorts each notice by type). The line is the Trade Policy Uncertainty index (Caldara and colleagues, Federal Reserve), built from newspaper coverage of trade-policy worries.
